Earthwork Volume Calculator

Cut and fill volume between two cross-sections using the average end area method - the standard for roads, site grading and drainage earthworks.

Average end area: V = L × (A1 + A2) / 2. Compacted fill shrinks, so the loose material you need to import is higher than the compacted volume. See the civil engineering guide for earthwork practice.

Brick Quantity Calculator

Number of bricks and mortar volume for a wall, using Pakistani standard brick size (9" x 4.5" x 3") with mortar joints.

Rule of thumb: about 500 bricks and 0.3 m³ of mortar per m³ of brickwork (joints included). Bricks counted as 500/m³ nominal, plus wastage. See the civil engineering guide for masonry practice.

Plaster Quantity Calculator

Cement and sand for wall and ceiling plaster by area, thickness and mix ratio - with the dry-volume allowance.

Dry volume = wet volume × 1.33 (sand bulking + voids). Cement assumed 1,440 kg/m³; a 50 kg bag is about 0.0347 m³.

Asphalt Quantity Calculator

Volume and tonnage of asphalt for roads, driveways and pavements by area and compacted thickness.

Typical compacted asphalt density 2,300 - 2,400 kg/m³. Ordering is usually by tonne; add 4-8% for wastage and tack coat overlap. See roads & pavements in the civil guide.

Aggregate & Sand Quantity Calculator

Crush, bajri and sand needed for concrete and mortar by volume or by member quantities.

Uses dry-volume factors: concrete 1.54, mortar 1.33. For 1:2:4 concrete, cement/sand/aggregate are 1/7, 2/7, 4/7 of the dry volume. See concrete & masonry in the construction guide.

Rate Analysis Calculator

Break a BOQ item into material, labour and plant to justify a rate for extras and new items.

Direct cost = material + labour + plant. Rate = direct cost x (1 + overhead%) / quantity. Overhead usually covers supervision, scaffolding, tools and profit. See rate analysis service.

Rebar Estimator by Member

Approximate reinforcement weight for slabs, beams, columns and footings from member dimensions.

Reference steel contents per m³ of concrete: slab 70-110 kg, beam 120-180 kg, column 150-220 kg, footing 60-100 kg. Actual depends on design loads, spans and detailing. See structural design services.
FAQ

Common questions

How many cement bags are in 1 m³ of M15 (1:2:4) concrete?

About 6.9 bags of 50 kg each (dry volume allowance 1.54, 5% waste). The mix calculator above reports it for any volume and mix.

How much does a 12 mm rebar weigh per metre?

Approximately 0.888 kg/m (using d²/162, where d is in mm). The rebar calculator covers all common sizes in kg/m, per-bar and totals.

Are these engineering calculators free?

Yes - 100% free, no sign-up, and everything runs in your browser. No files are uploaded anywhere.

Can I use these results for final design?

These are reference and preliminary tools. Final structural design must follow applicable codes and be checked by a licensed engineer - contact Struc-Arch Pakistan for design support.

How is earthwork volume measured for roads?

By cross-section. Each station gets a measured cross-section showing cut and fill areas; volume between adjacent sections uses the average end area method (V = L × (A1 + A2) / 2). Compacted fill shrinks, so the loose material needed is higher than the compacted volume - the earthwork calculator above handles both.

How many bricks are in one m³ of brickwork?

About 500 for Pakistani standard bricks (9" x 4.5" x 3") with mortar joints, plus 0.3 m³ of mortar. A 9" wall needs roughly 115 bricks per square metre of wall face (about 57 for a 4.5" half-brick wall).

How much plaster covers a 50 kg bag of cement?

About 11 m² at 12 mm thick with a 1:4 mix, and roughly 7 m² at 19 mm. The plaster calculator above reports exact cement and sand for any area, thickness and mix.

How is asphalt quantity ordered for a road?

By tonne. Multiply the compacted volume by the asphalt density (about 2,350 kg/m³) and add 4-8% for wastage and overlaps. For a 100 m² driveway at 50 mm you need roughly 12 tonnes.
